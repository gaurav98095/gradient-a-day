Vision Transformer (ViT) turns an image into a sequence of 16×16 patches, then sends those patches through a standard Transformer. Unlike CNNs, which grow their receptive field layer by layer, self-attention can connect distant patches from the start. A learned **[CLS]** token collects the image representation for classification.

The recipe is simple: flatten each patch, project it into an embedding, add position information, and apply Transformer blocks. The paper shows that, with large-scale pre-training, pure Transformers can beat strong CNNs while using fewer training resources. ViT comes in Base, Large, and Huge sizes; bicubic interpolation helps adapt position embeddings when image resolution changes. The main lesson: enough data lets attention’s weaker visual inductive bias become a strength.
